Robot BOM builder: a bill of materials from your photos

Photos → evidence-linked parts → your review → verified bill of materials. Every line points back to what was seen in a photo, the critical parts nobody could see get a clearly tagged best guess, and nothing counts as confirmed until you say so.

1. Add photos of your robot

A top view plus an underside view and close-ups of board labels work best. Up to 8 images. Parts that are not visible are never assumed.

Drop photos here, or click to choose JPG, PNG or WebP · you can also paste an image from the clipboard

Your photos

2. Visible components

Seen in at least one photo. Confirm or reject each line, correct counts, and enter make / model / SKU only if you verified them on the part. Empty means unknown, not zero.

3. Unverified / likely hidden

Not seen in any photo. These are possibilities and open questions — not confirmed parts.

4. Issues and compatibility checks

Rule-based critical issues close only when the component data is corrected (specs such as voltage_v=7.4; current_a=1.2). Warnings can be ticked off with a note.

AI best guess: settle every issue and find the parts

Runs automatically after every analysis. The model picks the most likely design choice for every open issue and infers the critical parts nobody could see (drive motors, motor driver, power source, controller). Those guesses are written into the tables above with an AI guess tag, and the checks are re-run on them, so “unknown” issues close. Each part is then looked up on the approved shops by web search, and the pages found are checked for stock. Guesses stay “needs review”: only you can confirm a line. After editing the tables, use the button to run it again.
